Here's the uncomfortable truth: motivation is a feeling, and feelings come and go. The people who get things done aren't more motivated than you — they've just stopped relying on motivation. Here's how to do the same.

## 1. Lower the bar to start

The hardest part is starting, so make starting tiny. "Write one sentence." "Do one push-up." "Open the file." Once you begin, momentum usually carries you further than you planned.

> Action creates motivation far more often than motivation creates action.

## 2. Build systems, not goals

A goal ("get fit") is a wish. A system ("workout Mon/Wed/Fri at 7am") removes the daily decision. When the choice is already made, you don't need to feel motivated — you just follow the plan.

## 3. Make it obvious and easy

Lay out your gym clothes the night before. Put the book on your pillow. Reduce the friction between you and the thing you want to do, and you'll do it more.

## 4. Track the streak

Marking an X on a calendar each day you show up creates a chain you won't want to break. The streak itself becomes the motivation.

## 5. Forgive the off day — then return

Everyone misses a day. The difference between people who succeed and people who quit is simple: **never miss twice.** One off day is a blip; two is the start of a new (worse) habit.

## The bottom line

Stop waiting to feel ready. Shrink the first step, build a system around it, and show up imperfectly. Discipline isn't about feeling motivated — it's about not needing to.
