Kyoto rewards the unhurried. Rush it and you'll see a checklist of temples; slow down and you'll feel why people fall in love with it. Here's how to spend three gentle days.

## Day 1 — East Kyoto (Higashiyama)

Start before 8am. The lanes around **Kiyomizu-dera** are magical when empty and unbearable by 10am. Walk down through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka, two preserved slopes lined with wooden tea houses.

> Tip: Most temples open at 6–8am. The first hour is the difference between a photo and a fight for one.

Afternoon: wander the **Philosopher's Path**, a canal-side walk dotted with small cafés.

## Day 2 — Arashiyama & bamboo

Take the early train west to **Arashiyama**. The bamboo grove is famous for a reason, but the real find is the quiet temple gardens just past it.

- Get there by 8:30am
- Bring cash for small shrines
- Save the afternoon for a riverside lunch

## Day 3 — markets & tea

Spend your last morning at **Nishiki Market** ("Kyoto's kitchen") tasting your way through pickles, tofu and matcha. End with a proper tea ceremony — book one a day ahead.

## What I'd skip

You don't need every temple. Pick three that matter to you and actually sit in them. That's the whole point of slow travel.
